Website review · October 2026

Fair Tourism website redesign: what changed and why

An interactive web and mobile prototype for fairtourism.nl. It keeps the Foundation's voice, colours and Poppins typeface, and reorganises the site around what visitors come to do: understand the work, support it, partner with the team or learn about community tourism.

Main improvements

The biggest changes are in structure and content, not decoration.

The prototype reuses the existing brand. Most of the work went into making information easier to find, easier to trust and easier to act on.

Navigation named after what visitors want to do

About · What we do · Our work · Our impact · Resources, with “Donate now” as the primary button and “Partner with us” as a clearly secondary one.

Current site: “Our Work” lists services, “Our Projects” opens a page titled “Our Impact”, and two different addresses show the same page.

A homepage that routes visitors by intent

Three entry points (discover projects, explore a partnership, support the work) and the five-step method from the two-pager: Consider, Conceive, Craft, Connect, Conserve.

Current site: eleven stacked sections with several repeated calls to action; visitors work out for themselves where to start.

Project pages that lead with facts

A quick-facts bar at the top (project lead, location, period, focus, scale), then why it matters, key challenges and activities, approach, goals, film and a community voice.

Current site: the same facts exist but sit in a side box further down the page.

Research and training made visible

TOURLINK (EU-funded, 2020–2024) and the Travelife human rights training now have their own pages under Our work, with links to the published research.

Current site: both are small tiles on the projects page and easy to miss.

An Our impact page that is careful with numbers

The eight figures from the two-pager are grouped into four snapshots. Each states what it counts and carries a “reporting period to confirm” note. Figures with different definitions are never charted against each other.

Current site: there is no dedicated impact page.

One searchable resource library

  • Insights, blog posts and reports in one place, filtered by four topics and searchable
  • Thirteen articles migrated with full text, author and date
  • Six items load at a time, then “Show more resources”
  • The two-page brochure and the six partner logos sit below the list

One design system across web and mobile

  • Shared type scale, spacing, colours, buttons and form states
  • Every page checked at phone width with no sideways scrolling
  • Visible keyboard focus and descriptive image text
  • Gentle float-in motion and a pausable hero film, both switched off when visitors ask for reduced motion

Donation page with the purpose beside the form

Four uses of funds, each with concrete activities, sit next to the amount selector. Amounts match the current Donorbox form (€10, €50, €100 or another amount, one-time or monthly), so the block can connect to the existing Donorbox and Stripe setup.

Site structure

From seven menu items to five, plus two clear actions.

The menu now follows the questions visitors ask: who are you, what do you offer, where do you work, what has changed, and where can I read more.

Issues found

Worth fixing on the current site, whether or not the redesign goes ahead.

Content

  • The Travelife page shows an internal editing note: “Insert call to action button linking it to Kim de Leew”, followed by a staff email address.
  • The Sasak project page lists “X amount increase in visitors” as a key challenge.
  • The Thailand project film is a general South-East Asia travel montage, not footage from Huay Pu Keng.
  • Mission and “Our Movement” on the About page use identical text.
  • The Inspire & Educate FAQ repeats the needs-assessment questions.

Links and technical

  • Two TOURLINK research links point to the same Google Drive folder through a personal account path (/drive/u/0/), which outside visitors may not be able to open.
  • In the footer, “Knowledge Programs” and “Impact Reports” link to the Impact Reporting service page.
  • All partner logos use “Pink Poppy Flowers” as their image description.
  • A draft page, /new-about-page, is listed in the sitemap and can appear in search results.

Before launch

What the team needs to confirm or provide.

The prototype marks every unverified figure, date and quote as “to confirm” rather than presenting it as fact.

Facts and permissions

  • Reporting period and definition for each impact figure
  • Project start dates (Sasak: 2024 in the two-pager, 2025 on the project page)
  • Current team list and photo permissions
  • Consent for community photos, quotes and films; replacement film for Huay Pu Keng
  • Public access to the TOURLINK research files

Content and build

  • Key challenges for the Sasak project; FAQ answers for the service pages
  • Replace the 84 sample entries in the resource library, which demonstrate browsing 100 items
  • Connect the donation block to the existing Donorbox form
  • Connect the contact and newsletter forms, with file upload for volunteer applications
  • Record a baseline in website analytics before launch so the redesign can be measured
